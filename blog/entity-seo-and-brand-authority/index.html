Entity SEO: How to Become a Brand AI Systems Recognize

Published September 21, 2026 by Wesley Hopkins

Ask an AI assistant "who is [your business]?" and see what comes back. Some owners are pleasantly surprised. Many find a vague description, the wrong city, a competitor's services, or nothing at all. Every one of those outcomes has the same root cause: the systems aren't sure who you are.

That problem has a name, entity SEO, and it's less mysterious than it sounds.

What an "entity" is

An entity is a distinct, identifiable thing: a person, a company, a product, a place. Search engines and language models increasingly reason about entities rather than strings of text. To them, "Apex Roofing" isn't a keyword. It's a candidate for a specific business, and they have to decide which business, if any, a given page or mention refers to.

Entity SEO is the work of making that decision easy and correct. You provide clear, consistent, verifiable facts about who you are, and you make sure other credible sources say the same things.

Where E-E-A-T fits

You've probably heard of E-E-A-T: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness. It comes from Google's quality rater guidelines, the criteria human evaluators use to judge search quality. Google has said it isn't a direct ranking factor, but it describes what Google's systems are trying to reward, so it's a useful frame for what "trustworthy" looks like from the outside.

Entity work is how you make E-E-A-T visible to machines. A great expert with no findable credentials, no consistent presence, and no third-party validation looks like an unknown. The steps below fix that.

Step 1: Get your basic facts consistent everywhere

Pick the exact form of your business name, address, phone number, and one-sentence description. Then make them match across your website, your Google Business Profile, social profiles, and every directory that lists you. Small inconsistencies (a suite number here, "LLC" there, an old phone number) add up to real ambiguity.

Write the description once, and reuse it: "Example Plumbing is a licensed residential plumbing company serving Springfield, Illinois" should read the same on your homepage, in your schema, and in your profiles. That repetition is the point.

Step 2: Build a real About page

Your About page is the anchor for your entity. It should say plainly who runs the business, what it does, where it operates, how long it's been operating, and how to contact it. Include real names and faces. Link out to your official profiles. Anonymous "our team of experts" copy tells a machine, and a human, nothing to hold onto.

Step 3: Give your content named, credentialed authors

Every article should carry a real byline that links to an author page: who they are, what they've done, what they're qualified to speak about, and where else they appear. For advice topics where accuracy matters (legal, medical, financial, home safety), this isn't decoration. It's the difference between advice a system can trust and text of unknown origin. Add Person and Article markup that matches (see Schema Markup for AI Search).

Step 4: Declare your official profiles

Use sameAs in your Organization schema to list the profiles that officially represent you: LinkedIn, Facebook, industry directories, your Google Business Profile, and Wikidata or Wikipedia if you qualify. This is how you tell a machine "these accounts are all the same entity," which helps it connect scattered mentions back to you.

Step 5: Earn third-party corroboration

This step matters most and can't be done alone. AI systems read what the wider web says about you: reviews, news coverage, directory listings, podcasts, association memberships, forum threads. A business that's described consistently by several independent sources looks real and reliable. One that's only described by itself doesn't.

Realistic ways to earn it:

  • Reviews from real customers, on the platforms your customers use.
  • Local and industry press, trade publications, and association directories.
  • Guest appearances: podcasts, webinars, community events, conference speaker lists.
  • Partners and suppliers who list you as a client or vendor.
  • Original research or data others cite because it isn't available elsewhere.

A warning, because the shortcut is tempting. Google's AI guidance says outright that pursuing inauthentic mentions "isn't as helpful as it might seem." Fake reviews are worse than unhelpful: the FTC finalized a rule in 2024 banning them, and the damage to trust is hard to undo. We wrote about how the manufactured web is eroding trust in Inside the Manufactured Web. The durable strategy is boring: do good work, ask satisfied customers to say so, and be findable when they do.

Step 6: Claim a knowledge panel and structured presence, if you qualify

Larger and more established entities may earn a Google Knowledge Panel and a Wikidata entry. Don't force this. Wikipedia and Wikidata have notability and neutrality requirements, and self-promotional pages get deleted. If you do meet the bar (real independent coverage exists), those sources are heavily used as reference points. If you don't, invest in the steps above until you do.

Step 7: Audit what AI currently says about you

Before and after your changes, check the output directly. Ask the major assistants variations of:

  • "Who is [business name]?"
  • "What does [business name] do?"
  • "Is [business name] reputable?"
  • "Best [your service] in [your city]" (see if you appear at all)

Write down what's wrong, then trace each error to a source. A wrong address usually traces to a stale directory listing. A missing service usually traces to a page that doesn't say it plainly. Fix it at the source, because that's where the systems learned it. You can't edit an AI's answer directly, and asking it to change won't stick.

Because answers vary from run to run, repeat each question a few times and look at patterns. The process for doing this systematically is in How to Track Your Visibility in AI Search.

A quick entity checklist

  • Business name, address, and phone identical across your site and every profile
  • One canonical description reused everywhere
  • A real About page with named people
  • Bylines and author pages on every article
  • Organization schema with a stable @id and accurate sameAs
  • Complete, current Google Business Profile
  • A steady stream of genuine reviews
  • Coverage or mentions on at least a few independent, credible sites
  • Periodic checks of what AI assistants say about you

Entity work is the connective tissue of the AI Search Visibility guide: it's what turns good content and clean technical setup into a brand systems will actually recommend.
